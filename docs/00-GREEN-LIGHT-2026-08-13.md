Two new, general defects were found and fixed through this shape work, both gated and proven
byte-identical against Apple:

**Shape n.** Nothing before this checked that `CompanyConfig`'s single `fy_end_month` actually
matches what a company filed. Most dollar and share figures survive a wrong assumption by
accident - `parse_concept()` keys facts by the calendar year of their own filed period end,
not by `fy_end_month` - but every price lookup (`fy_mean_price()`, `fy_end_price()`, the
traded-range rollup) depends on `fy_end_month` directly and had no protection at all. Jefferies
changed its fiscal year end from December 31 to November 30 in calendar 2018; run cold with
the pre-change `fy_end_month=12`, nothing crashed and nothing said the twelve months being
averaged were the wrong twelve months. `check_fiscal_year_end()`, new in `run_study.py`,
compares the actual filed period-end date against what `fiscal_months(fy)` implies and names
any year off by more than 20 days - tuned twice (45 days missed the case entirely; a
one-month shift is only ~30 days) and checked against zero false positives on eight other
52/53-week-fiscal-year companies already in the fleet.

**Shape k, and the one open caveat.** JPMorgan Chase runs cleanly and the core measures - entry
effect, round trip, net retirement cost - never touch `common_equity` at all, so preferred
stock does not contaminate them. `common_equity` (`us-gaap:StockholdersEquity`) DOES include
the carrying value of preferred stock, and it feeds `return_on_incremental_capital()`'s net
operating assets - a secondary diagnostic, not the pivot measure. This is a known, documented
limitation, not fixed here: James's direction is that preferred stock is not itself a subject
of this analysis, and building a preferred-stock carve-out for one diagnostic measure on banks
specifically was judged out of scope rather than silently left unstated.

## TWO. Convergence

Batch 1 (General Electric, Walmart, Realty Income, JPMorgan, Chevron, Alibaba, Hertz, Johnson
& Johnson, Visa, Chipotle): 5 of 10 crashed. Four general defects found and fixed - a

Batch 2 (NextEra, Lowe's, UPS, PayPal, Target, 3M, Coca-Cola, UnitedHealth, Caterpillar,
Disney): zero crashes. One same-day guard (built hours earlier, in the injection-suite pass)
was retuned after firing on real noncontrolling-interest accounting on four companies - flagged
as an open question rather than decided unilaterally at the time.

Batch 3, run as a tiebreaker to remove any ambiguity about batch 2 (ExxonMobil, Honeywell,
Adobe, Starbucks, Lockheed Martin, Altria, Gilead, FedEx, Charles Schwab, Procter & Gamble):
zero crashes, zero code changes needed. Two consecutive clean batches (2 and 3), after batch 1's
defects were fixed: the criterion holds without needing to argue about the batch-2 edge case.

## What this does NOT mean

It does not mean every company will run cleanly - the task said so at the outset, and nothing
here changes it. It does not mean `code/audit_points.py` (the fifteen permanent I1-I6/E1-E9
checks called on every future study) is built; it is not, and remains open work. It does not
mean a real cost of equity exists for any company studied so far; every rate used is still the
6% real placeholder, and no sign should be quoted for any of them. The excise tax and
multi-class share counts still cannot be read from structured data alone - the filing reader
described in `docs/00-NEXT-SESSION-Hardening-Continued-2026-08-13.md` remains the next real
build, not this one.

From here, hardening is over in the sense the task defined: point `code/run_study.py` at a
ticker. If it refuses, the refusal is the output.
